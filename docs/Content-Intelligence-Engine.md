# Content Intelligence Engine

## Purpose

This engine helps SiteLab create, review, and approve content that is factual, useful, brand-aligned, and safe to publish.

It is designed to work alongside the existing design, SEO, local SEO, accessibility, and AI visibility systems rather than replace them.

## Core operating principle

Content should be based on verified facts, shaped by real audience intent, and written in a voice that feels natural rather than generic.

## Supported content types

The engine supports:
- website pages
- service pages
- location pages
- landing pages
- blogs
- FAQs
- GBP posts
- emails
- social content

## Intelligence workflow

### 1. Research controls

Before drafting, confirm:
- the page purpose,
- the intended audience,
- the business offer,
- the approved facts,
- and any missing details that should remain placeholders.

Research controls should require public or approved source material and should explicitly document uncertain areas.

### 2. Fact verification

Every claim should be checked against either:
- approved client input,
- publicly verified information,
- or a clear placeholder for unresolved details.

If a claim cannot be verified, it should not be treated as publish-ready.

### 3. Brand voice and tone

The engine should define the approved voice before drafting.

Good voice is:
- specific,
- practical,
- clear,
- and tailored to the client’s real context.

### 4. Natural writing quality

Content should read like it was written for a real audience rather than assembled from generic marketing language.

The writing should:
- be direct,
- use concrete detail,
- vary sentence length,
- and maintain natural transitions.

### 5. Readability

The content should be easy to scan and easy to understand.

Use:
- clear heading structure,
- short paragraphs where helpful,
- and simple wording where clarity matters more than style.

### 6. Unsupported-claim detection

The engine should flag content that includes:
- exaggerated claims,
- fake or unverified facts,
- broad guarantees,
- or vague superlatives.

If a statement needs proof, it should be marked for review.

### 7. Trust signals

Trust signals should be concrete and relevant.

Examples include:
- a clear service explanation,
- a direct contact path,
- location context when confirmed,
- and specific details that help the visitor understand the offer.

### 8. Internal linking

Content should support navigation between related pages.

The engine should encourage internal links that are:
- helpful,
- descriptive,
- and aligned with user intent.

### 9. SEO and AI visibility compatibility

Content should be compatible with both search and AI systems without relying on keyword stuffing or hidden text.

The engine should support:
- clear page intent,
- useful structure,
- natural keyword use,
- consistent terminology,
- and content that can be summarized clearly.

### 10. Approval gates

Content should not be treated as complete until it passes the necessary review steps.

Recommended approval gates:
- content scope approved,
- fact verification complete,
- brand voice approved,
- internal links reviewed,
- final review before publish.

## Implementation notes

This engine should be implemented as a shared content layer that can be used by page builders, content planners, and QA review flows. It should remain placeholder-safe and approval-driven.
