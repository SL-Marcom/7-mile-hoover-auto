# Human Writing Standard

## Purpose

This standard defines how SiteLab content should sound when it is written for real audiences.

The goal is to produce copy that feels clear, concrete, and trustworthy rather than generic, repetitive, or overly polished.

## Writing rules

### Avoid

- unnecessary em dashes,
- repetitive AI-style phrases,
- generic filler,
- exaggerated claims,
- fake or unsupported facts,
- and repetitive sentence structures.

### Favor

- concrete language,
- varied sentence length,
- natural transitions,
- client-specific terminology,
- and approved brand voice.

## Practical guidance

### Use clear language

Prefer words that describe the real offer, the real audience, and the real next step.

### Keep the rhythm natural

Varied sentence length helps the writing feel more human and less mechanical.

### Be specific

Say what the business does, who it serves, and what the visitor should do next.

### Avoid unsupported claims

Do not imply awards, guarantees, rankings, or results that have not been verified and approved.

### Keep the tone grounded

The writing should sound confident and useful, not inflated.

## Review checklist

Before finalizing content, confirm that it:
- avoids unnecessary dash punctuation,
- does not rely on repetitive filler phrases,
- contains specific language rather than vague marketing words,
- uses verified facts or clear placeholders,
- and aligns with the approved voice.
