# Client Intelligence Engine

> This workflow is operationalized as reusable, typed code in the [Client Intake Engine](Client-Intake-Engine.md) (`src/lib/client-intake`). Use that engine to actually gather, validate, classify, and store intake information; use this document for the underlying discovery, research, and decision methodology.

## Purpose

This document defines the intelligence system SiteLab uses before every website project begins.

The purpose of this engine is to determine what should be built, what must be confirmed, what should be researched, and what should be deferred until approval.

It is a pre-build decision system. It exists to reduce guesswork, protect quality, and ensure that websites are based on real client context rather than assumptions.

## Core operating principle

No implementation should begin until the project has enough verified information to justify the build.

That means:
- research must come first,
- assumptions must be tested,
- missing information must be clearly marked,
- and major decisions must be presented for approval before implementation.

## Intelligence workflow overview

The engine operates in five stages:

1. Discovery
2. Research
3. Synthesis
4. Decision
5. Approval gate

Each stage produces a deliverable that helps determine what should be built.

## 1. Client discovery workflow

The discovery workflow gathers the minimum reliable information needed to frame the project.

### Discovery objectives
- understand the client’s business,
- identify the website’s purpose,
- determine the desired conversion path,
- identify available facts versus missing facts,
- and define what needs approval before implementation.

### Discovery questions
SiteLab should ask questions that clarify:
- what the business does,
- who the ideal customer is,
- what the website should help visitors do,
- what success looks like,
- what information is already available,
- what must be confirmed,
- and what should remain placeholder content.

### Discovery outputs
The discovery process should produce:
- a client summary,
- a project objective statement,
- a list of confirmed facts,
- a list of missing facts,
- and a first-pass page plan.

### Discovery rules
- Ask direct questions before making assumptions.
- Capture uncertainty explicitly.
- Mark unknowns as placeholders rather than filling them with invented content.
- Do not assume business claims, locations, service areas, reviews, credentials, pricing, or guarantees.

## 2. Business understanding framework

The business understanding framework translates raw client input into a usable project model.

### Business understanding should answer:
- What does the business actually do?
- Who is the ideal customer?
- What is the primary user need?
- What action should the site help the user take?
- What makes this business credible?
- What information is missing and should not be inferred?

### Business understanding categories
- Business type
- Core offer
- Primary audience
- Primary conversion goal
- Geographic context
- Service scope
- Trust requirements
- Content constraints

### Business understanding rules
- Use only facts that are either provided or publicly verifiable.
- If facts are unverified, keep them unconfirmed until approved.
- If the business is local, respect local context without overstating coverage or reach.

## 3. Research workflow

Research should happen before implementation and should be grounded in public information first.

### Research objectives
- validate assumptions,
- gather public context,
- identify likely user expectations,
- understand the market and category,
- and collect evidence that can support planning decisions.

### Research sources
Use public information sources such as:
- the client’s existing website or materials, if provided,
- public business listings or official web presence, if relevant and approved,
- public-facing business information that is clearly attributable,
- competitor websites for category understanding,
- and publicly available local context where appropriate.

### Research rules
- Research public information first.
- Refrain from using private, speculative, or unverified information.
- Present findings for review before incorporating them into the site strategy.
- Never rely on invented facts to fill gaps.

## 4. Competitor analysis methodology

Competitor analysis should clarify the market context without encouraging imitation.

### Competitor analysis goal
The goal is to understand patterns in the category and identify what users expect, not to copy competitor structure or content.

### Competitor analysis should examine:
- common page structure,
- common trust elements,
- common CTA patterns,
- service or offer framing,
- content tone,
- local relevance signals,
- and conversion patterns.

### Competitor analysis outputs
- category patterns,
- differentiation opportunities,
- trust expectations,
- likely user questions,
- and a list of design and content directions that may be useful.

### Competitor analysis rules
- Use competitor research to inform strategy, not to recreate competitor layouts.
- Avoid copying copy, positioning, messaging, or visual structure too closely.
- Highlight what is useful and what should be avoided.

## 5. Target audience identification

The target audience defines who the site is intended to serve and how content should be framed.

### Audience dimensions
- primary audience,
- secondary audience,
- likely intent,
- likely concern,
- likely decision trigger,
- and likely device context.

### Audience outputs
- audience summary,
- user intent summary,
- key questions the audience likely has,
- conversion context,
- and copy direction.

### Audience rules
- Use evidence-based assumptions.
- If audience data is weak, keep the strategy broad and flexible until confirmed.

## 6. Business goals classification

Every project should be classified by its primary business goal.

### Goal categories
- Generate leads
- Encourage calls or direct contact
- Capture bookings or appointments
- Provide service information
- Support local discovery and directions
- Reinforce trust and credibility
- Support sales or quote requests

### Goal rules
- One primary goal should be identified clearly.
- Secondary goals should be explicitly documented.
- The implementation should prioritize the primary goal.

## 7. SEO strategy decision process

SEO strategy should be determined based on real business context and intent.

### SEO decision questions
- Is the business local and location-dependent?
- Is the site intended to rank for service-based queries?
- Does the business need specific service pages or location pages?
- Is there already a public presence that can support basic authority?
- Is the business in a category where content depth matters?

### SEO strategy outputs
- page prioritization,
- page structure recommendations,
- keyword intent strategy,
- internal linking needs,
- content priorities,
- and metadata planning.

### SEO rules
- Do not overpromise rankings.
- Base the plan on the available business context and approved content.
- Use content and structure that support discoverability without manipulative tactics.

## 8. AI Visibility strategy decision process

AI Visibility should be treated as an intentional content and structure concern.

### AI Visibility decision questions
- What is the page trying to communicate clearly?
- How should the business and services be described?
- Which terms and entities are most important?
- How should the content be structured for clarity and summarization?

### AI Visibility outputs
- entity naming guidance,
- content structure direction,
- key service wording,
- and internal-link clarity.

### AI Visibility rules
- Write content that is clear and factual.
- Avoid keyword stuffing or misleading claims.
- Make the page easy for both users and AI systems to understand.

## 9. Google Ads readiness assessment

Google Ads readiness should be assessed before any tracking or campaign-related implementation is considered.

### Readiness questions
- Is the site’s conversion path clear?
- Is the landing page aligned with the campaign goal?
- Is the content and CTA aligned with the offer?
- Are there clear approval-ready pages and conversion points?
- Is the business ready for tracking and measurement if approved?

### Readiness outputs
- campaign alignment assessment,
- landing page readiness,
- conversion path clarity,
- and tracking prerequisites if approved.

### Readiness rules
- Do not install analytics or advertising-related scripts without explicit approval.
- Do not treat ads readiness as permission to deploy or publish.

## 10. Local SEO assessment

Local SEO assessment determines whether local relevance should be a major part of the site strategy.

### Local SEO decision questions
- Is the business location-specific?
- Is the business serving a defined service area?
- Are the business details already confirmed?
- Are directions or local discovery important?

### Local SEO outputs
- local relevance strategy,
- page needs for service areas or locations,
- and a list of details that require confirmation.

### Local SEO rules
- Use local information only when it is real and approved.
- Do not assume service areas or coverage if they have not been confirmed.

## 11. Design direction decision framework

The design direction framework determines the visual strategy before implementation begins.

### Design decision questions
- What kind of experience should the site create?
- Is the business more editorial, premium, practical, warm, technical, or direct?
- What mood should the site signal?
- What visual references are inspiring but not copied?
- What trust cues are necessary?
- What visual rhythm is appropriate?

### Design direction outputs
- design tone,
- content hierarchy strategy,
- section pacing direction,
- visual emphasis plan,
- and a list of required trust elements.

### Design rules
- The design must feel custom-made and original.
- Avoid default template-looking layouts.
- Use inspiration as a source of principles, not a source of duplication.

## 12. Required website pages

The page plan should be based on the business goal and sitemap needs.

### Typical pages
- Home
- Services or offerings
- About
- Contact
- Directions or location page, if relevant
- Booking or quote page, if relevant
- FAQ, if useful

### Page planning rules
- Do not add pages just to fill the site.
- Every page should have a clear purpose.
- The page set should support the primary conversion goal.

## 13. Trust element selection

Trust elements must be selected based on the business and the likely concerns of the visitor.

### Common trust elements
- clear service descriptions,
- direct contact information,
- simple proof points,
- location context,
- response clarity,
- business summary,
- accessible forms,
- and clear next steps.

### Trust rules
- Trust elements must be grounded in real, approved information.
- Do not invent testimonials, credentials, awards, or guarantees.

## 14. CTA strategy

CTA strategy should be derived from the business goal and visitor intent.

### CTA logic
- primary CTA should support the main conversion path,
- secondary CTA should support alternative intent,
- and the page should avoid competing actions that dilute focus.

### CTA rule
- The site should make the next step obvious and easy.

## 15. Approval gate before implementation

No implementation should begin until the intelligence work has been reviewed and approved.

### Approval gate requirements
The following should be reviewed and approved before implementation:
- client summary,
- business understanding,
- research findings,
- page plan,
- primary CTA and secondary CTA,
- trust element plan,
- page requirements,
- and design direction.

### Implementation rule
- If important information is missing, the project should pause until it is clarified.
- If the project depends on assumptions, those assumptions should be explicitly documented and approved.

## Decision standard: when enough information is collected

Enough information has been collected when:
- the site’s purpose is clear,
- the primary conversion goal is clear,
- the required pages are understood,
- the core trust and CTA strategy is clear,
- the missing information has been identified,
- and the implementation plan is ready for review.

If those conditions are not met, the project is not yet ready to build.

## Research and validation discipline

SiteLab should validate before building:
- confirm what is known,
- identify what is unknown,
- separate verified facts from assumptions,
- and present the plan for approval.

This protects the project from fabricated content, weak strategy, and premature implementation.

## Output of the engine

The intelligence engine should produce a concise pre-build brief containing:
- project objective,
- audience and intent,
- confirmed facts,
- open questions and placeholders,
- page plan,
- CTA strategy,
- trust elements,
- SEO and AI Visibility direction,
- design direction,
- and implementation approval status.
