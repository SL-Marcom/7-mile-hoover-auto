# Local SEO Intelligence Engine

## Purpose

This engine provides shared local SEO planning and review guidance for the SiteLab operating system.

It extends the existing content, SEO, AI visibility, and industry knowledge architectures rather than creating a parallel workflow.

## Scope

The engine covers:
- Google Business Profile strategy
- business category planning
- NAP consistency
- storefront vs service-area logic
- single-location vs multi-location logic
- service areas
- location page planning
- local landing page planning
- local search intent
- local entity consistency
- local schema recommendations
- local internal linking
- local trust signals
- review and reputation strategy
- citation planning and consistency checks
- map and directions guidance
- city and neighborhood targeting
- duplicate/thin location page prevention
- local content quality review
- local AI visibility
- approval gates

## Integration model

The Local SEO Intelligence Engine should consume the existing content blueprint, SEO blueprint, AI visibility blueprint, and industry knowledge blueprint where appropriate so it stays aligned with page intent, content strategy, and machine-readable clarity. Industry knowledge should only ever supply candidate categories and typical trust signals for review, never confirmed business facts.

It should not invent addresses, service areas, reviews, categories, locations, business hours, or GBP details. Instead, it should require verified public research and owner confirmation before using business facts.

## Core operating rules

- Keep local plans grounded in confirmed facts and public research.
- Use placeholders when business details are pending or unverified.
- Separate storefront, service-area, and multi-location logic clearly.
- Avoid thin or repetitive local pages.
- Keep local content useful and specific to the user’s immediate need.
- Treat local SEO as a review requirement, not a publishing shortcut.

## Approval gates

A local SEO plan should not be treated as ready until it passes:
- business fact review,
- location model review,
- service-area review,
- local schema and internal-link review,
- and final local SEO approval.
